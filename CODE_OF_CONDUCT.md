# Code of Conduct

## The standard

Be someone people can learn from and work with.

That is the whole principle. Everything below is elaboration for the cases where it is not obvious.

## Why this project needs one specifically

This repository is aimed substantially at beginners, and beginners are the people most easily driven
away from technology. A single dismissive answer to a basic question does more damage than any typo,
because the person who asked stops asking.

It also takes technical positions, some of them unfashionable, and disagreement about them is
expected and welcome. The distinction that matters: **arguing about a technical claim is the work;
arguing about a person is not.**

## Expected behaviour

**Assume the question is reasonable.** If someone does not know something, they are in the target
audience. "Have you read the docs?" is not an answer, and "what specifically did the docs not make
clear?" is.

**Criticise claims, not people.** "This overstates what a hash table guarantees, because collisions
make the worst case O(n)" is useful. "This is amateur work" is not, and it is also less informative.

**Be specific.** Vague criticism cannot be acted on and reads as hostility even when none is
intended. Name the file, the claim, and what you think is true instead.

**Accept being wrong.** Everyone here will be corrected. The correct response is "you're right,
fixed" and then moving on — no self-flagellation required, and no defence of a position you no longer
hold.

**Give the reasoning.** Especially when reviewing. "Change this" costs the author a guess; "change
this, because a reader at Level 1 will not have the prerequisite for that explanation" teaches them
something.

**Respect the time of volunteers.** Maintainers are not obliged to review on your schedule, and a
polite follow-up after a week is fine.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained hostility toward anyone.
- Demeaning, insulting, or derogatory comments; personal or political attacks.
- Discriminatory language or conduct relating to any personal characteristic.
- Sexual language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing others' private information without permission.
- Deliberately derailing discussions, or reopening settled decisions without new arguments.
- Gatekeeping: implying that someone does not belong here because of what they do not yet know. This
  is the one most likely to occur in a project like this one, and it is the most damaging.

## Scope

Applies in all project spaces — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and any
project-related communication — and when representing the project publicly.

## Enforcement

Report concerns to the repository maintainers via a private channel, or by opening an issue if the
matter is not sensitive. Reports will be reviewed and responded to; details of any report will not be
shared beyond those handling it.

Consequences are proportionate and escalate: a private correction, a public correction, a temporary
ban, a permanent ban. Maintainers who do not follow or enforce this in good faith may face
consequences determined by other maintainers.

## A note on technical disagreement

This project makes claims that some experienced engineers will disagree with — that microservices are
usually the wrong default, that certificates are weak signals, that grinding problem sets is a poor
use of time.

**Those disagreements are welcome and are not conduct issues.** Bring an argument, evidence, or a
case the current text does not handle, and the text will change if you are right. What is not welcome
is treating a disagreement about content as a reason to be contemptuous toward the person who wrote
it.
